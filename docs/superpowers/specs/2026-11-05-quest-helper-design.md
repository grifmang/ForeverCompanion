# Forever Companion quest helper

Status: Approved on November 5, 2026. Target: World of Warcraft: Forever (Camelot), as a module in the existing Forever Companion add-on.

## What players will do

1. On arriving in a zone, open **Nearby** in the tracker. See nearby quest givers and the offers at each stop. Sort stops by distance when comparable and show the next pickup on the map on click. When objective locations are known, label which route area each pickup adds work to and highlight bundles that share a work area, even when the quests come from different givers. Show quests that the installed data and character information suggest are available; distinguish these from offers confirmed by the live NPC dialogue. The player talks to the NPC and accepts quests normally.
2. Open **Route** to see all accepted quests grouped where their current work can be done. Click **Track** on an area. The tracker highlights the closest actionable quest in that section, shows its objective text and progress, and requests native map guidance where the client supports it. Keep other quests in the section visible, so players can finish overlapping objectives together.
3. When the current quest becomes ready for turn-in, put it in **Turn in** and advance to the closest remaining quest in the selected section. If only the work in this section is complete, advance within the section and leave the unfinished quest listed in its other areas. Recompute from the player's position at that moment. A player can select a different section, pick a specific quest, or stop routing. Keep the selected target stable as the player moves; only change it on a route action, a reliable quest or objective transition, or when a target is removed. Once the section is finished, show that state and its ready turn-ins. Let the player decide when to switch sections.
4. While an **Adventure Together** session is active, display the same party progress next to each quest, including how many participants are ready and which reports are unknown. Show the session title and checklist progress from the tracker, with a link to the existing Adventure Together controls. Group progress informs the player and does not override their route.

## Where quests go

- Prefer the map and subarea of the current unfinished objective. One quest can appear in more than one area if it has confirmed unfinished objectives in both; count it once in any total across areas. Use a stable area key, not the displayed name. Group by zone when reliable subarea data is absent.
- Keep ready turn-ins, instance quests, and quests with no reliable objective location visible in their own sections. A starting NPC and the quest log category are not evidence of where a quest objective takes place. Use the actual hand-in location for completed objectives when available.
- Rank current eligible targets by straight-line distance in comparable world space, not percentages on different maps. When possible, use native objective waypoints and position data. Supplemental points can describe multiple spawns; choose the closest valid candidate. Mark approximate locations and make no claim about walkable paths, travel time, or cross-continent distance. When coordinates or current quest state are unreadable, preserve the quest and show the limitation instead of inventing a distance, removing the quest, or jumping to an unknown target.
- Discovery starts with the current mapped area and nearby quest hubs. Filter with readable character data and known prerequisites, level, faction, race/class, repeatability and quest history. Omit known ineligible quests. Unverified restrictions or coverage can yield **Likely available**, never a guarantee. Exclude completed non-repeatables and accepted quests; update after NPC dialogue, acceptance, turn-in, level change and zone change. Display the actual number of accepted quests against the native limit when readable.

## Data and compatibility

Blizzard describes more than 1,000 new quests and several new or reimagined zones. The Classic quest catalog alone won't cover Forever. Its Classic and Enhanced experience presets also differ in native quest POIs and objective blobs. Respect the player's settings and keep the text route useful when pins or waypoints are unavailable. [Blizzard's launch notes](https://worldofwarcraft.blizzard.com/en-us/news/24315820/world-of-warcraft-forever-goes-live-globally-november-4)

- Use live Camelot quest log data for local progress, objectives and readiness. Evaluate `C_QuestLog.GetNextWaypoint` and native quest POI data for known destinations, and `C_Map.GetPlayerMapPosition` plus `C_Map.GetWorldPosFromMapPos` for distance. Confirm the APIs, argument types, update ordering and real availability in the launch client before relying on them. Native client guidance tracks one selected target; use owned state so ending the route restores the prior tracking choice if it is still valid and the user hasn't changed it. Keep existing watch-list entries. [Forever quest log API](https://github.com/Gethe/wow-ui-source/blob/forever/Interface/AddOns/Blizzard_APIDocumentationGenerated/QuestLogDocumentation.lua) · [Forever map API](https://github.com/Gethe/wow-ui-source/blob/forever/Interface/AddOns/Blizzard_APIDocumentationGenerated/MapDocumentation.lua)
- Use an optional, separately installed **QuestieDB Forever** provider for broad discovery and supplemental locations, subject to verifying its released client flavor, consumer API, availability fields and coordinate mapping. Link setup from **Nearby** when the provider isn't installed or compatible. In that case, offer client-confirmed local NPC offers when readable and keep accepted-quest routing usable wherever native data exists. Call out missing coverage, especially in Forever-specific content. Read the installed data at runtime; do not copy provider code or datasets into Forever Companion. No in-game HTTP, no auto-accept, and no automation of player movement or quest actions. [QuestieDB public API](https://github.com/Questie/QuestieDB/blob/master/docs/api.md) · [Forever data coverage](https://github.com/Questie/QuestieDB/blob/master/docs/forever-data.md) · [License](https://github.com/Questie/QuestieDB/blob/master/LICENSE.md) · [Precedent and eligibility limits](https://github.com/TylerAkins/forever-quest-markers)
- Give the quest helper and Adventure Together independent on/off settings. Move local quest log reading to one shared lifecycle that runs if either feature needs it; keep Adventure Together's existing roster, protocol, opt-out and stale-report behavior. Turning off sharing must not disable solo routing. Turning off the helper restores Blizzard's tracker and the existing Adventure Together decorations. Keep route choice and view preferences local; don't send location, discovery suggestions, route selections, or additional packets to the party.

## Look and tracker replacement

Use the native Forever Objective Tracker as the visual reference: compact translucent dark panel, native fonts, gold headers, familiar quest markers, red-gold hover controls, and indented objective text. Default to the usual HUD position under the minimap. The active target gets one clear highlight; location, party readiness and state have text labels as well as icons. Use compact collapsible headers, a scrollable list for full logs, and native tooltips. Long titles wrap, and the panel stays readable over light terrain and at different UI scales. Keep movement and effects restrained.

The new panel is the single everyday HUD tracker when enabled, not a second window next to Blizzard's quest list. Keep the Quest Log and World Map. Preserve native actions such as opening quest details and using a quest item. Account for non-quest objectives and game states (for example tracked achievements, instance objectives, and protected item or controller actions). Use supported native UI paths for those surfaces; where the client will not safely expose an equivalent, temporarily hand the HUD back to Blizzard with an explanation. Do not reparent or repurpose protected frames. Offer a one-action **Use Blizzard Tracker** escape hatch. Defer unsafe changes in combat and recover after leaving combat; never trap the player with both trackers hidden. Enable replacement only after the live client compatibility path has been verified.

Visual evidence: Forever's [tracker frame](https://github.com/Gethe/wow-ui-source/blob/forever/Interface/AddOns/Blizzard_ObjectiveTracker/Blizzard_ObjectiveTracker.xml) uses a managed layout and Edit Mode; its [container](https://github.com/Gethe/wow-ui-source/blob/forever/Interface/AddOns/Blizzard_ObjectiveTracker/Blizzard_ObjectiveTrackerContainer.xml) specifies the primary-header art, native header font, collapse controls and NineSlice frame. Take the progressive disclosure pattern from the [21st accordion collection](https://21st.dev/community/components/explore/accordion-component), not its visual style. [Magic UI's Animated List](https://magicui.design/docs/components/animated-list) and [React Bits' Animated Content](https://reactbits.dev/animations/animated-content) informed keeping attention on the changed row; don't animate the whole list or copy those components. Uiverse returned 403; Mobbin's public catalog didn't expose a relevant detailed flow. No web UI libraries or assets are required.

## Acceptance in WoW Forever

Use one representative leveling route with a nearby giver offering several quests, objectives in two areas, a ready turn-in, and a quest with unknown location. Walk it in the launch client to verify discovery, acceptance, grouping by where the work happens, distance order, manual selection, automatic advance, completing overlapping objectives, restoring native tracking and the usable no-provider path. Check both client presets where they affect guidance. Exercise tracker hide/restore in combat, instances and an unsupported native activity; verify quest item access, map actions, and HUD input on the live client. Add a second character to the same session to verify shared progress, missing/stale reports and opt-out. Use static Lua parsing and release package validation for the build. Do not add or run unit or integration tests; report anything that could not be exercised in the live client.

If a logged-in launch client is unavailable, a packaged build can be handed off for the walkthrough, but live replacement behavior remains unverified and the replacement must stay off by default. The browser preview uses sample data and does not verify WoW behavior.
